Dual Subtitles Plus - YouTube & Netflix

Two subtitle languages at once — one above the video, one below.

Watch YouTube and Netflix with dual subtitles in two languages at once. Display the translation at the top of the video and the original subtitles at the bottom, or place both at the bottom. Keep the two subtitle lines from overlapping.

Add to Chrome

No account · No API key · Nothing is sent anywhere

Above: translation at the top, original at the bottom. Below: both lines stacked at the bottom.

The popup: choose the language, layout and text size.

More settings: colours, backdrop and distance from the bottom.

What it does

  • Dual subtitles — see the video's original subtitles and a second language together.
  • Flexible layout — translation above and original below, or both stacked at the bottom.
  • Customisable display — adjust text size, colours and position. Your language and display preferences are remembered across videos.
  • No extra account or API key required.

How it works

Original subtitles — YouTube & Netflix: uses the subtitles available on the video. If no subtitles are available, the extension lets you know.

Translated subtitles — YouTube: uses YouTube's built-in translation. Netflix: uses Netflix's subtitles in your chosen language when available. If not, Chrome translates the available subtitles directly on your device where supported (39 languages).

Getting started — YouTube: no additional setup. Netflix: open the extension once, select "Turn on for Netflix" and grant access.

Translation quality depends on YouTube, Netflix or Chrome, not on this extension.

Privacy

This extension collects nothing.

  • No personal information, browsing history, or usage data is collected.
  • No data is transmitted to the developer or to any third party. There is no server.
  • Your preferences (languages, layout, text size, colours) are stored locally with the browser's own storage API and never leave the device.
  • The extension has access to youtube.com. Access to www.netflix.com is optional: Chrome asks for it only when you press “Turn on for Netflix” in the popup, and you can decline. It requests no access to any other site.
  • On Netflix, a language Netflix does not offer is translated on your device by Chrome's built-in translator. Subtitles are not sent anywhere.
  • No advertising, no tracking pixels, no remote code.

Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to request, correct, or delete. Removing the extension removes its stored settings with it.

This website — not the extension — uses Google Analytics to count visits, video plays and clicks through to the Chrome Web Store. The extension itself collects nothing.

Questions

How do I show two subtitles at the same time on YouTube?
Open a video that has subtitles, then pick a language under “Translate to” in the extension popup. Nothing else to set up.
Can I put the translation on top and the original at the bottom?
Yes, or stack both at the bottom.
Can I change the subtitle size, colours and position?
Yes. In the popup, choose “Layout” and “Text size”. For more, click “More settings” in the popup: a separate colour for the original and the translation, backdrop opacity, and the distance from the bottom.
Will my settings stay the same on the next video?
Yes. Your languages, layout, size and colours stay the same on the next video, on YouTube and on Netflix.
Do I need an account or an API key?
No.
Does it send my subtitles or data anywhere?
No. There is no server and no analytics; your settings stay in your browser.
Does it work on videos with only auto-generated subtitles?
Yes. But subtitles YouTube generates automatically translate less accurately than subtitles written by the creator.
How do I get dual subtitles on Netflix?
The first time, press “Turn on for Netflix” in the extension popup and allow access. After that, it works on its own. Chrome asks for Netflix access only at that moment, and you can decline — YouTube works without it.
Why don't two subtitle lines appear on a Netflix title?
“Turn on for Netflix” in the popup hasn't been pressed yet, the title has no subtitles, or its subtitles are images rather than text, which can't be read. The popup tells you which one it is.
What if Netflix doesn't have subtitles in my language?
Chrome translates the subtitles on your device (39 languages, where supported). This needs an up-to-date Chrome. The first time you use a language, Chrome downloads it once.
Does it work on Disney+, Prime Video or other sites?
No. It works on YouTube and Netflix only.
How is it different from Language Reactor?
Dual Subtitles Plus is built to be simple. Pick a language and two subtitle lines appear on YouTube and Netflix — no account, no API key, nothing new to learn. An easy choice for learners who want to study with dual subtitles.
Does it work on smartphones?
No. It is an extension for Chrome on a computer.
Does it slow the player down?
The subtitle tracks are fetched once per video and rendered locally, so playback is unaffected.
Can I turn it off for a while?
Yes — the switch at the top of the popup. YouTube's own captions behave normally again the moment it is off.
